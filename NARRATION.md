## Eight AI ideas for SNITCH’s marketing team

Eight AI ideas for Snitch’s marketing team. More from every shoot, with a clearer path from content to customers.

## Eight ideas. One marketing workflow.

These eight ideas connect the work Snitch already does.

Keep Hotline moving.

Help customers style and shop.

Create with the AI Edit Studio.

See performance together.

Check before publishing.

Explore new photoshoot directions.

Adapt campaigns for stores.

Extend shoots to YouTube.

## Hotline content engine

First, Hotline. Consistent posting needs a production rhythm, rather than a daily chase.

A planner reads the weekly brief, asset library and product feed, then proposes seven posts.

The edit engine prepares previews. Copy and sound helpers add grounded captions and licensed music.

A person approves, edits or swaps; the publisher rechecks stock, schedules, and returns results to next week’s plan.

Use it to keep the queue moving while the team spends its time on the campaign, rather than coordinating every post.

## Hotline AI stylist in DMs

Second, the Hotline stylist. Interest in a look is the moment to help someone shop.

A STYLE comment starts an opening private reply; the customer replies before conversation continues.

The stylist asks occasion, budget and preference. Catalogue tools check real products, sizes, prices and stock.

It suggests looks, refines them, then offers confirmed product links or store options.

Use it to turn engagement into a useful shopping journey: from liking an outfit to finding a look and knowing where to buy it.

## AI Edit Studio · MVP

Third, the Edit Room. Every shoot gives Snitch more material than one campaign can use.

Give the engine footage, a brief and a reference. It finds usable takes and proposes an editable cut.

Sound, graphics and colour build around the cut. The editor inspects the timeline and proposes changes before anything is approved.

The editor approves. A searchable library preserves the work; format recipes prepare channel and screen versions.

Here are the selected final films, including Campaign Mix. These are real MVP outputs. Choose any film to play it in full. Use the engine after shoots to reuse footage and prepare more versions, while the editor stays in control.

## Performance dashboard, every handle

Fourth, one performance dashboard. Separate handles should not mean separate answers about what works.

With permission, a collector saves account results beside each post and its production effort.

The engine compares similar campaigns, keeping source records attached. Missing data stays unknown.

A weekly summary recommends what to repeat, improve or stop, feeding the next brief.

Use it in the Monday review and budget discussion: connect creative output, production effort and audience response to the next decision.

## Pre-publish QC + performance read

Fifth, the Hook Lab. A finished edit can still lose viewers before its message lands.

The engine checks framing, sound, text, opening and pacing, using specific frames as evidence.

It returns practical re-cut notes: what to change, where, and why. A person chooses.

A trial reel tests the revision with non-followers; results guide whether to share, revise or stop.

Use it before release to sharpen the creative hypothesis, catch avoidable problems and learn from actual audience response.

## AI model + photoshoot studio

Sixth, the photo studio. Explore campaign directions around a real garment before committing to a full shoot.

Product photos define the garment. The brief sets the theme; approved references define the model.

Choose the identity first. The engine plans shots, generates candidates and assembles a grid of shots for review.

A stylist checks faces, colour, logos and garment details before approved images enter the library.

Use it for concepts and social variations, with the stylist protecting the product. Start with one campaign, measure quality and human effort, then expand what works.

## Store handles, adapted automatically

Seventh, local store content. One campaign can reach many stores without a complete edit for each.

The registry collects confirmed store details, language and usable facade footage.

The adapter changes approved opening and closing slots, local lockup and call to action, protecting the master’s timing and music.

A diff and quality check flag changes. Approve the campaign, spot-check versions, then stagger release.

Use it for local launches, seasonal campaigns and city-specific calls to action. Measure versions produced and time spent reviewing them.

## YouTube from the same shoot

Eighth, YouTube. The shoot is already paid for; its footage can live beyond Instagram.

An approved film enters the engine. Templates prepare Shorts, titles, descriptions and thumbnails.

The team reviews privately, with a named person approving anything scheduled for release.

Results return to the dashboard, guiding the next package and creating a repeatable channel rhythm.

Use it after campaign shoots to reach another audience without starting another production, while carrying the learning into the next release.

## One studio. A connected growth loop.

Together, these eight ideas form one loop, rather than eight separate tools.

The Edit Studio and photo studio create reusable assets. The Hook Lab checks them; people approve.

Hotline, YouTube and store versions distribute that work. The stylist turns product interest into a shopping journey.

The dashboard returns results to the next brief. The shared impact: faster creative turnaround, consistent content, more value from each shoot, and better-informed decisions.
